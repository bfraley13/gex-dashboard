How to trade gamma

Today's playbook: NQ 28,543 is 1,514 pts below the Flip (30,057): negative gamma. Lean toward trend and breakout trades with wider stops, and expect moves to extend. Downside magnets are the Near Put Wall 28,094 and the 1D low 28,130. A reclaim of 30,057 would flip the regime to positive.
  1. Regime first. Below the Flip = negative gamma: dealers amplify moves, so favour trend and breakout trades and use wider stops. Above the Flip = positive gamma: price tends to mean-revert, so fade the edges of the range and take quicker targets.
  2. Walls are targets and turn points. The Call Wall acts as a ceiling, the Put Wall as a floor. Price often stalls or reverses there, but if it is accepted through a wall (holds beyond it), the move often speeds up.
  3. The Flip is the line in the sand. Reclaiming it or losing it shifts the regime, and the playbook above flips with it.
  4. Trade confluence, not single lines. The best spots are where a gamma level lines up with a 1h supply/demand zone, the POC or VWAP. Even then, wait for a 1-minute trigger (Strat 2-2 or a VWAP hold) before entering.
  5. 1-day expected move (NQ 28,130 – 28,956, ±412 pts). An early tag of the min or max is stretched: look for a reversal there, don't chase.
  6. Timing. Levels matter most in the first 1–2 hours of the session and around expirations. Big news (CPI, FOMC, NFP) can override them.

Method

Black-Scholes vanna and charm from Schwab IV (r = 4%, no dividend), the same inputs as gamma. Dealer sign as GEX: dealers assumed long calls (+) and short puts (−). Vanna exposure = sign × OI × 100 × vanna × S × 1% = dollars of dealer delta added per +1 IV point; when IV falls 1 pt dealers trade about +vanna exposure (positive = they buy). Charm exposure = sign × OI × 100 × charm/365 × S = dollars of dealer delta added per day passing; dealers re-hedge by about −charm exposure (positive = they sell). The charm target is the strike within ±1.5% of price with the largest nearest-expiry charm, where decay-driven hedging concentrates. Flow uses Schwab totalVolume vs prior-day OI; Schwab does not report trade side.

Method

Black-Scholes vanna and charm from Schwab IV (r = 4%, no dividend), the same inputs as gamma. Dealer sign as GEX: dealers assumed long calls (+) and short puts (−). Vanna exposure = sign × OI × 100 × vanna × S × 1% = dollars of dealer delta added per +1 IV point; when IV falls 1 pt dealers trade about +vanna exposure (positive = they buy). Charm exposure = sign × OI × 100 × charm/365 × S = dollars of dealer delta added per day passing; dealers re-hedge by about −charm exposure (positive = they sell). The charm target is the strike within ±1.5% of price with the largest nearest-expiry charm, where decay-driven hedging concentrates. Method

Black-Scholes vanna and charm from Schwab IV (r = 4%, no dividend), the same inputs as gamma. Dealer sign as GEX: dealers assumed long calls (+) and short puts (−). Vanna exposure = sign × OI × 100 × vanna × S × 1% = dollars of dealer delta added per +1 IV point; when IV falls 1 pt dealers trade about +vanna exposure (positive = they buy). Charm exposure = sign × OI × 100 × charm/365 × S = dollars of dealer delta added per day passing; dealers re-hedge by about −charm exposure (positive = they sell). The charm target is the strike within ±1.5% of price with the largest nearest-expiry charm, where decay-driven hedging concentrates.
